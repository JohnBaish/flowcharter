**GUIDE**
# How Flowcharter works

Flowcharter turns a list into a flowchart. Answer a few questions one step at a time, or type your steps as a list, and the chart is drawn for you. There's no dragging boxes about. (You can return to this intro page at any time via “Guide”.)

**GETTING STARTED**
## Two ways to build a chart

Press New chart and choose Step by step or Start with a blank list. Both build the same chart, and you can swap between them whenever you like.

Step by step asks what happens next, in plain English: something gets done, you ask a question, information comes in or goes out, or it's the end. Write the step, and it's added to your chart. When you ask a question, the cards follow each answer in turn until every path has an ending. On a computer the chart grows beside the cards; on a phone, tap Peek at the chart. Press Back to change what you've just written.

Switch to building in List mode takes you to the List at any point, and the List's Step by step button brings you back to the cards while anything is still unfinished.

**THE LIST**
## Writing the steps yourself
- Add one row for each step: press + Add item, or press Enter in a box.
- The Type decides the step's shape. Flowcharter guesses (the first row is the start, and a row ending in a question mark is a question), and you can change it.
- A question's answers appear underneath it. Use goes to to say where each one leads: pick a step, or + New step to add one. Pointing back at an earlier step makes a loop.
- Every other row leads on to the next one. To skip ahead, go back or stop, change its then box.

**THE CHART**
## Seeing and sharing it

Use the List | Chart switch at the top to see your chart. Automatic draws it top to bottom or left to right, whichever fits your screen better; the other button lets you choose the other way. Print it, or use Copy image to paste it into something else.

**THE KEY**
## The shapes

Each type of step has its own shape and its own colour.
- Start — Start / End — Where the process begins or finishes. One start, as many ends as needed.
- Book the backup — Action — Something that gets done. The wording usually starts with a verb.
- Backupavailable? — Question — A question. Each arrow leaving it is labelled with an answer.
- Receive call — Data in/out — Information coming in to, or going out of, the process.
- Arrange cover — Standard routine — A routine that has its own separate flowchart elsewhere.
- Rota — Document — A document or report is produced or used.
- Wait an hour — Delay — A waiting period where nothing happens.

The shapes follow the usual flowchart conventions (ISO 5807). The colours are Flowcharter's own, and a chart still makes sense printed in black and white.

**MAKING IT CLEAR**
## Tips
- Start each action with a verb: "Send reminder", not "Reminder".
- Write questions that have short answers, such as Yes or No.
- Keep the wording short. Long text makes big boxes and a chart that's harder to read.
- Use the rarer shapes only when they add something; most charts need just the first four.

**YOUR FLOWCHARTS**
## Where they're kept

Your flowcharts are saved automatically, but only in this browser, on this device. Use Export on the Library to keep a backup or to move them to another device, and Import to bring them back.

The flowcharts you make here are not collected; they're only saved in your browser. We collect anonymous visit statistics (no cookies, no personal data) to see how the site is used. Flowcharter is a free-to-use personal project by John Baish. Contact: flowcharter@baish.net
